5.1

energy conservation

work done

work is done when a force causes an object to move through a distance in the direction of the force.

$$W=Fs$$

  • W = work done (J)
  • F = force (N)
  • s = displacement in the direction of the force (m)

one joule is the work done when a force of one newton moves an object through one metre.

principle of conservation of energy

energy cannot be created or destroyed. it can only be transferred from one form to another or transformed from one form into another.

therefore, the total energy of an isolated system remains constant.

  • chemical energy → electrical energy
  • electrical energy → light and heat
  • gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy

efficiency

efficiency is the ratio of useful energy output to the total energy input.

$$\text{Efficiency}=\frac{\text{Useful Energy Output}}{\text{Total Energy Input}}$$

efficiency is often expressed as a percentage.

$$\text{Efficiency (\%)}=\frac{\text{Useful Energy Output}}{\text{Total Energy Input}}\times100$$

  • efficiency is always less than or equal to 100%.
  • some energy is always dissipated, usually as heat or sound.

power

power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.

$$P=\frac{W}{t}$$

  • P = power (W)
  • W = work done or energy transferred (J)
  • t = time taken (s)

one watt is equal to one joule of work done every second.

power and motion

when an object moves at constant speed under the action of a force, power can also be expressed as:

$$P=Fv$$

  • F = force (N)
  • v = velocity (m s-1)

this equation is useful for calculating the power of moving vehicles, motors and engines.

important notes

  • work is done only when there is displacement.
  • the displacement must be in the direction of the force.
  • energy is always conserved.
  • no machine is 100% efficient.
  • power measures how quickly work is done.
  • use \(P=Fv\) only when force and velocity act in the same direction.
