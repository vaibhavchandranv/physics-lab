5.2

gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy

gravitational potential energy

gravitational potential energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its position or height in a gravitational field.

when an object is raised, work is done against gravity. this work is stored as gravitational potential energy.

$$\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h$$

  • \(\Delta E_p\) = change in gravitational potential energy (J)
  • \(m\) = mass (kg)
  • \(g\) = gravitational field strength (N kg-1)
  • \(\Delta h\) = change in height (m)

on Earth, the gravitational field strength is approximately 9.8 N kg-1 (often taken as 10 N kg-1).

derivation of gravitational potential energy

work done is given by:

$$W = Fs$$

when lifting an object vertically, the force required equals its weight.

$$F = mg$$

the displacement is the change in height, \(\Delta h\).

$$W = mg\Delta h$$

since the work done is stored as gravitational potential energy,

$$\boxed{\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h}$$

kinetic energy

kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its motion.

$$E_k=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

  • \(E_k\) = kinetic energy (J)
  • \(m\) = mass (kg)
  • \(v\) = velocity (m s-1)

kinetic energy increases with both mass and the square of velocity.

derivation of kinetic energy

work done is given by:

$$W=Fs$$

from Newton's second law,

$$F=ma$$

substituting,

$$W=mas$$

using the equation of motion,

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

for an object starting from rest,

$$u=0,\qquad v^2=2as$$

$$as=\frac{v^2}{2}$$

therefore,

$$W=m\left(\frac{v^2}{2}\right)$$

$$\boxed{E_k=\frac12 mv^2}$$

important notes

  • gravitational potential energy depends on mass, gravity and height.
  • kinetic energy depends on mass and the square of velocity.
  • doubling the mass doubles both gravitational potential energy and kinetic energy.
  • doubling the velocity increases kinetic energy by four times.
  • both forms of energy are measured in joules (J).
