7.5

polarisation

what is polarisation?

polarisation is the restriction of the oscillations of a transverse wave to a particular direction.

because polarisation requires the oscillations to be restricted to a particular direction, it is a phenomenon associated with transverse waves.

longitudinal waves cannot be polarised because their oscillations are already parallel to the direction of propagation.

plane-polarised electromagnetic waves

an electromagnetic wave is plane-polarised when its electric field oscillates in one fixed direction perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

a polarising filter only allows the component of the electric field parallel to its transmission axis to pass through.

malus's law

when a plane-polarised electromagnetic wave passes through a polarising filter, the transmitted intensity depends on the angle between the polarisation direction of the incident wave and the transmission axis of the filter.

$$\boxed{I=I_0\cos^2\theta}$$

  • \(I\) = transmitted intensity after the filter
  • \(I_0\) = intensity of the plane-polarised incident wave
  • \(\theta\) = angle between the incident polarisation direction and the transmission axis of the filter

this relationship is known as malus's law.

special cases

when the transmission axis is parallel to the polarisation direction, \(\theta=0^\circ\).

$$I=I_0\cos^2(0^\circ)=I_0$$

therefore, the intensity is unchanged.

when the transmission axis is perpendicular to the polarisation direction, \(\theta=90^\circ\).

$$I=I_0\cos^2(90^\circ)=0$$

therefore, no light is transmitted.

when \(\theta=45^\circ\),

$$I=I_0\cos^2(45^\circ)=\frac{1}{2}I_0$$

therefore, half of the incident intensity is transmitted.

series of polarising filters

malus's law can be applied repeatedly when a plane-polarised wave passes through a series of polarising filters.

after the first filter,

$$I_1=I_0\cos^2\theta_1$$

the intensity after the next filter is then

$$I_2=I_1\cos^2\theta_2$$

and so on for additional filters.

for two filters, this can be combined into

$$\boxed{ I_2=I_0\cos^2\theta_1\cos^2\theta_2 }$$

here, \(\theta_2\) is the angle between the transmission axes of the two filters, while \(\theta_1\) is the angle between the original polarisation direction and the first filter's transmission axis.

worked example

a plane-polarised electromagnetic wave has an intensity of \(80\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}\). it passes through a polarising filter whose transmission axis is at \(30^\circ\) to the direction of polarisation. find the transmitted intensity.

using malus's law,

$$I=I_0\cos^2\theta$$

$$I=80\cos^2(30^\circ)$$

$$I=80\left(\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)^2$$

$$\boxed{I=60\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}}$$

important limitation

the calculation of the intensity after a polarising filter is required when the incident wave is already plane-polarised.

calculations involving the effect of a polarising filter on an unpolarised wave are not required for this syllabus.

summary

  • polarisation is a phenomenon associated with transverse waves.
  • a plane-polarised electromagnetic wave has its electric field oscillating in one fixed direction.
  • a polarising filter transmits the component of the electric field parallel to its transmission axis.
  • malus's law is \(I=I_0\cos^2\theta\).
  • \(\theta\) is the angle between the incident polarisation direction and the transmission axis.
  • malus's law can be applied successively to a series of polarising filters.
  • calculations for an unpolarised incident wave are not required.
