7.1

progressive waves

wave motion

wave motion is the transfer of energy and information from one place to another by a travelling disturbance, without the net transfer of matter.

in a progressive wave, the disturbance moves through the medium while particles of the medium oscillate about their equilibrium positions.

examples of wave motion can be seen in a vibrating rope, a vibrating spring, and ripples on the surface of water.

displacement

displacement is the distance and direction of a particle from its equilibrium position.

displacement varies with time as the particle oscillates about its equilibrium position.

amplitude

amplitude is the maximum displacement of a particle from its equilibrium position.

amplitude is represented by \(A\) and is measured in metres (m).

phase difference

phase describes the position of a particle within its cycle of oscillation. phase difference describes how far apart two particles are in their cycles.

two particles are in phase when they have the same displacement from equilibrium and are moving in the same direction at the same time.

two particles separated by one complete wavelength are in phase.

two particles separated by half a wavelength have a phase difference of \(180^\circ\), or \(\pi\) radians.

$$\Delta\phi = 2\pi\frac{\Delta x}{\lambda}$$

  • \(\Delta\phi\) = phase difference (rad)
  • \(\Delta x\) = separation between the particles (m)
  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength (m)

period

the period \(T\) is the time taken for one complete oscillation.

$$T=\frac{1}{f}$$

  • \(T\) = period (s)
  • \(f\) = frequency (Hz)

frequency

frequency \(f\) is the number of complete oscillations made per second.

$$f=\frac{1}{T}$$

the SI unit of frequency is the hertz (Hz).

wavelength

wavelength \(\lambda\) is the shortest distance between two adjacent points on a wave that are in phase.

for example, the distance between two adjacent crests or two adjacent troughs is one wavelength.

wavelength is measured in metres (m).

wave speed

wave speed \(v\) is the speed at which the wave disturbance travels.

during one period, the wave travels a distance of one wavelength. therefore,

$$v=\frac{\lambda}{T}$$

since

$$f=\frac{1}{T}$$

the wave equation follows:

$$\boxed{v=f\lambda}$$

  • \(v\) = wave speed (m s\(^{-1}\))
  • \(f\) = frequency (Hz)
  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength (m)

energy transfer

a progressive wave transfers energy from one point to another.

the particles of the medium themselves do not travel with the wave. instead, they oscillate about their equilibrium positions and transfer energy to neighbouring particles.

therefore, a wave can transfer energy without producing a net transfer of matter.

intensity

intensity is the power transferred by a wave per unit area perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

$$I=\frac{P}{A}$$

  • \(I\) = intensity (W m\(^{-2}\))
  • \(P\) = power transferred (W)
  • \(A\) = area (m\(^{2}\))

for a progressive wave, the intensity is proportional to the square of its amplitude:

$$I\propto A^2$$

therefore, if the amplitude is doubled, the intensity becomes four times greater.

the cathode-ray oscilloscope

a cathode-ray oscilloscope (CRO) displays a changing electrical signal as a graph of potential difference against time.

the vertical axis represents potential difference, while the horizontal axis represents time.

the y-gain determines the potential difference represented by each vertical division on the screen.

the time-base determines the time represented by each horizontal division.

finding amplitude using a CRO

measure the vertical distance from the equilibrium position to the peak of the waveform in divisions.

multiply this by the y-gain:

$$V_{\text{amplitude}} = (\text{number of vertical divisions}) \times (\text{y-gain})$$

for example, if the peak is \(3\) divisions from the equilibrium position and the y-gain is \(2\,\mathrm{V\,div^{-1}}\),

$$V_{\text{amplitude}}=3\times2=6\,\mathrm V$$

finding frequency using a CRO

measure the horizontal distance corresponding to one complete cycle of the waveform.

multiply this by the time-base to find the period:

$$T= (\text{number of horizontal divisions per cycle}) \times (\text{time-base})$$

the frequency is then found using

$$f=\frac{1}{T}$$

for example, if one cycle occupies \(4\) divisions and the time-base is \(0.5\,\mathrm{ms\,div^{-1}}\),

$$T=4\times0.5\,\mathrm{ms}=2.0\,\mathrm{ms}$$

$$f=\frac{1}{2.0\times10^{-3}} =500\,\mathrm{Hz}$$

summary

  • wave motion transfers energy without transferring matter overall.
  • displacement is the distance and direction from equilibrium.
  • amplitude is the maximum displacement from equilibrium.
  • phase difference describes the relative positions of particles in their cycles.
  • period is the time for one complete oscillation.
  • frequency is the number of oscillations per second.
  • wavelength is the shortest distance between adjacent points in phase.
  • the wave equation is \(v=f\lambda\).
  • a progressive wave transfers energy.
  • intensity is \(I=P/A\).
  • for a progressive wave, \(I\propto A^2\).
  • a CRO uses y-gain to measure amplitude and the time-base to determine period and frequency.
