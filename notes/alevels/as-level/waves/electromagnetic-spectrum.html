7.4

electromagnetic spectrum

electromagnetic waves

electromagnetic (EM) waves are waves consisting of oscillating electric and magnetic fields.

all electromagnetic waves are transverse waves. the electric field and magnetic field oscillate perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

electromagnetic waves do not require a medium and can travel through a vacuum.

speed in free space

all electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in free space, regardless of their frequency or wavelength.

$$\boxed{c=3.00\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}$$

the speed, frequency and wavelength of an electromagnetic wave are related by the wave equation:

$$\boxed{c=f\lambda}$$

  • \(c\) = speed of electromagnetic waves in free space (m s\(^{-1}\))
  • \(f\) = frequency (Hz)
  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength (m)

the electromagnetic spectrum

the electromagnetic spectrum is the complete range of electromagnetic waves, arranged according to wavelength or frequency.

from longest wavelength to shortest wavelength, the principal regions are:

$$\boxed{ \text{radio} \rightarrow \text{microwave} \rightarrow \text{infrared} \rightarrow \text{visible} \rightarrow \text{ultraviolet} \rightarrow \text{x-ray} \rightarrow \gamma\text{-ray} }$$

wavelength ranges

the principal regions of the electromagnetic spectrum occupy different ranges of wavelength. the boundaries between regions are approximate.

region approximate wavelength range
radio waves \(> 10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m}\)
microwaves \(10^{-3}\) to \(10^{-1}\,\mathrm{m}\)
infrared \(7\times10^{-7}\) to \(10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m}\)
visible light \(400\) to \(700\,\mathrm{nm}\)
ultraviolet \(10^{-8}\) to \(4\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}\)
x-rays \(10^{-11}\) to \(10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m}\)
\(\gamma\)-rays \(< 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}\)

these values are approximate because the boundaries between regions of the electromagnetic spectrum are not sharply defined.

visible light

visible light is the small region of the electromagnetic spectrum that can be detected by the human eye.

in free space, the wavelength range of visible light is approximately

$$\boxed{400\,\mathrm{nm}\leq\lambda\leq700\,\mathrm{nm}}$$

wavelengths near \(400\,\mathrm{nm}\) appear violet, while wavelengths near \(700\,\mathrm{nm}\) appear red.

frequency and wavelength

since all electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed \(c\) in free space,

$$c=f\lambda$$

frequency and wavelength are therefore inversely proportional:

$$f\propto\frac{1}{\lambda}$$

electromagnetic waves with shorter wavelengths have higher frequencies, while waves with longer wavelengths have lower frequencies.

key properties

  • all electromagnetic waves are transverse.
  • all electromagnetic waves travel at \(3.00\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) in free space.
  • electromagnetic waves can travel through a vacuum.
  • the electromagnetic spectrum ranges from radio waves to \(\gamma\)-rays.
  • shorter wavelength corresponds to higher frequency.
  • visible light has wavelengths from approximately \(400\) to \(700\,\mathrm{nm}\).

summary

  • electromagnetic waves are transverse waves.
  • all electromagnetic waves have the same speed \(c\) in free space.
  • \(c=3.00\times10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
  • the wave equation is \(c=f\lambda\).
  • the principal regions are radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, x-ray and gamma rays.
  • visible light occupies approximately \(400\text{–}700\,\mathrm{nm}\).
