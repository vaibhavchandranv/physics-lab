7.3

doppler effect for sound waves

the doppler effect

the doppler effect is the change in observed frequency of a wave caused by relative motion between the source and the observer.

in this section, the source of sound moves while the observer remains stationary.

the observed frequency is different from the frequency emitted by the source.

source moving towards the observer

when the source moves towards a stationary observer, each successive wavefront is emitted from a position closer to the observer than the previous one.

the wavefronts therefore become closer together in front of the source. this decreases the wavelength of the sound waves.

since the speed of sound in the medium remains approximately constant, the observer detects a higher frequency.

$$f_o > f_s$$

source moving away from the observer

when the source moves away from a stationary observer, each successive wavefront is emitted from a position further away from the observer.

the wavefronts therefore become further apart behind the source. this increases the wavelength of the sound waves.

the observer consequently detects a lower frequency.

observed frequency

for a source of sound moving relative to a stationary observer, the observed frequency is

$$\boxed{f_o=f_s\frac{v}{v\pm v_s}}$$

  • \(f_o\) = observed frequency (Hz)
  • \(f_s\) = source frequency (Hz)
  • \(v\) = speed of sound in the medium (m s\(^{-1}\))
  • \(v_s\) = speed of the source relative to the medium (m s\(^{-1}\))

the sign depends on the direction in which the source is moving relative to the observer.

choosing the sign

when the source moves towards the observer, use the negative sign:

$$\boxed{f_o=f_s\frac{v}{v-v_s}}$$

when the source moves away from the observer, use the positive sign:

$$\boxed{f_o=f_s\frac{v}{v+v_s}}$$

since \(v+v_s>v\), the observed frequency is less than the source frequency.

why the frequency changes

the source emits sound waves at frequency \(f_s\), but its motion changes the spacing between successive wavefronts.

when approaching the observer, the wavefronts are compressed, giving a shorter wavelength. when receding, the wavefronts are spread out, giving a longer wavelength.

because

$$v=f\lambda$$

and the speed of sound in the medium is approximately constant, a smaller wavelength corresponds to a higher observed frequency, while a larger wavelength corresponds to a lower observed frequency.

worked example

a source emits sound at \(500\,\mathrm{Hz}\) and moves towards a stationary observer at \(20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). the speed of sound is \(340\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). find the observed frequency.

since the source is approaching, use the negative sign:

$$f_o = 500\left(\frac{340}{340-20}\right)$$

$$f_o = 531.25\,\mathrm{Hz}$$

therefore, the observer detects a frequency of approximately \(531\,\mathrm{Hz}\), which is higher than the source frequency.

important limitation

the expression used here applies specifically to a moving source and stationary observer.

the syllabus does not require the case of a stationary source and moving observer.

summary

  • the doppler effect is a change in observed frequency caused by relative motion between source and observer.
  • for this syllabus, the source moves while the observer remains stationary.
  • an approaching source produces a higher observed frequency.
  • a receding source produces a lower observed frequency.
  • approaching source: \(f_o=f_s\frac{v}{v-v_s}\).
  • receding source: \(f_o=f_s\frac{v}{v+v_s}\).
  • the change occurs because the motion of the source changes the wavelength of the sound reaching the observer.
