physical quantities

1.1.1 physical quantities and units

  • all physical quantities consist of a numerical magnitude and a unit
  • the numerical value shows how large the quantity is
  • the unit shows the standard used to measure the quantity

examples of physical quantities

  • length measured in metres (m)
  • mass measured in kilograms (kg)
  • time measured in seconds (s)
  • electric current measured in amperes (a)

1.1.2 estimating physical quantities

estimation is used when an exact value is not required.

  • estimation relies on real-world knowledge
  • reasonable values are substituted into equations
  • the aim is to find an approximate calculated value

useful values for estimation

  • resistivity of copper: 1.68 × 10−8 Ω m
  • young modulus of steel: 1.9 × 1011 pa

estimating density

  • use common sense to decide if an object floats or sinks in water
  • objects that float have density less than water
  • objects that sink have density greater than water
  • density of water ≈ 1000 kg m−3
  • density of water ≈ 1 g cm−3

estimation often involves rounding values to one significant figure.
