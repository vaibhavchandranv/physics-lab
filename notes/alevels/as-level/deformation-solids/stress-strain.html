6.1

stress and strain

deformation of solids

deformation is the change in the shape or size of an object when a force is applied.

when a force acts on a solid, the particles are displaced from their equilibrium positions. if the force is small, the particles return to their original positions when the force is removed. if the force is large enough, the change may become permanent.

deformation can occur in different ways.

  • extension – the object becomes longer.
  • compression – the object becomes shorter.

load

a load is the external force applied to an object that causes deformation.

the SI unit of load is the newton (N).

extension and compression

extension is the increase in length of an object when it is stretched.

$$\text{extension}=L-L_0$$

  • \(L\) = final length (m)
  • \(L_0\) = original length (m)

compression is the decrease in length of an object when it is squeezed.

stress

stress is the force acting per unit cross-sectional area of a material.

for the same force, a smaller area experiences a greater stress.

$$\sigma=\frac{F}{A}$$

  • \(\sigma\) = stress (Pa)
  • \(F\) = applied force (N)
  • \(A\) = cross-sectional area (m2)

the SI unit of stress is the pascal (Pa), where

$$1\ \mathrm{Pa}=1\ \mathrm{N\,m^{-2}}$$

strain

strain is the fractional change in length of a material.

since it is the ratio of two lengths, strain has no unit.

$$\varepsilon=\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}$$

  • \(\varepsilon\) = strain
  • \(\Delta L\) = extension (m)
  • \(L_0\) = original length (m)

young modulus

young modulus is a measure of the stiffness of a material.

it is defined as the ratio of stress to strain, provided the material obeys hooke's law.

$$E=\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}$$

  • \(E\) = young modulus (Pa)
  • \(\sigma\) = stress (Pa)
  • \(\varepsilon\) = strain

a material with a larger young modulus requires a greater stress to produce the same strain, making it stiffer.
