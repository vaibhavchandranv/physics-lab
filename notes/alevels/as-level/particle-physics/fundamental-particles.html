11.2

fundamental particles

fundamental particles

a fundamental particle is a particle that is not known to be made from smaller particles.

quarks and leptons are fundamental particles. protons and neutrons, however, are not fundamental because they are made from quarks.

quarks

there are six flavours, or types, of quark:

quark symbol charge
up \(u\) \(+\frac{2}{3}e\)
down \(d\) \(-\frac{1}{3}e\)
strange \(s\) \(-\frac{1}{3}e\)
charm \(c\) \(+\frac{2}{3}e\)
top \(t\) \(+\frac{2}{3}e\)
bottom \(b\) \(-\frac{1}{3}e\)

quarks have charges that are fractional multiples of the elementary charge \(e\).

the corresponding antiquark has the opposite charge to its quark.

  • up quark: \(+\frac{2}{3}e\) → anti-up: \(-\frac{2}{3}e\)
  • down quark: \(-\frac{1}{3}e\) → anti-down: \(+\frac{1}{3}e\)

the same rule applies to the other four flavours.

protons and neutrons

protons and neutrons are not fundamental particles. they are hadrons made from quarks.

a proton consists of two up quarks and one down quark:

$$p=uud$$

its total charge is

$$Q=+\frac{2}{3}e+\frac{2}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e=+e$$

a neutron consists of one up quark and two down quarks:

$$n=udd$$

its total charge is

$$Q=+\frac{2}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e-\frac{1}{3}e=0$$

hadrons

a hadron is a particle made from quarks. hadrons are divided into two main groups: baryons and mesons.

baryons consist of three quarks.

$$\text{baryon}=qqq$$

protons and neutrons are examples of baryons.

mesons consist of one quark and one antiquark.

$$\text{meson}=q\bar q$$

therefore:

  • baryon → three quarks
  • meson → one quark + one antiquark

β⁻ decay and quarks

β⁻ decay occurs when a neutron changes into a proton. at the quark level, this happens because one down quark changes into an up quark.

$$d\rightarrow u+e^-+\bar{\nu}_e$$

before the decay, the neutron has the composition

$$n=udd$$

one of its down quarks changes into an up quark, giving

$$udd\rightarrow uud$$

the particle is therefore transformed from a neutron into a proton.

β⁺ decay and quarks

β⁺ decay occurs when a proton changes into a neutron. at the quark level, one up quark changes into a down quark.

$$u\rightarrow d+e^++\nu_e$$

before the decay, the proton has the composition

$$p=uud$$

one of its up quarks changes into a down quark, giving

$$uud\rightarrow udd$$

the particle is therefore transformed from a proton into a neutron.

leptons

electrons and neutrinos are fundamental particles called leptons.

unlike protons and neutrons, leptons are not made from quarks.

  • electron: \(e^-\)
  • electron neutrino: \(\nu_e\)
  • positron: \(e^+\), the antiparticle of the electron
  • electron antineutrino: \(\bar{\nu}_e\), the antiparticle of the electron neutrino

leptons do not form hadrons because hadrons are particles made from quarks.

summary

  • quarks are fundamental particles with six flavours: up, down, strange, charm, top and bottom.
  • up-type quarks have charge \(+\frac{2}{3}e\); down-type quarks have charge \(-\frac{1}{3}e\).
  • antiquarks have opposite charge to their corresponding quarks.
  • proton: \(uud\).
  • neutron: \(udd\).
  • baryon: three quarks.
  • meson: one quark and one antiquark.
  • in β⁻ decay, \(d\rightarrow u+e^-+\bar{\nu}_e\).
  • in β⁺ decay, \(u\rightarrow d+e^++\nu_e\).
  • electrons and neutrinos are fundamental particles called leptons.
