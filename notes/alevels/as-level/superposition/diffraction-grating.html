8.4

the diffraction grating

diffraction grating

a diffraction grating consists of a large number of equally spaced parallel slits. when light passes through the slits, the diffracted waves from each slit interfere with one another.

constructive interference occurs at particular angles, producing sharp bright maxima.

the condition for a principal maximum is

$$\boxed{d\sin\theta=n\lambda}$$

  • \(d\) = separation between adjacent slits (m)
  • \(\theta\) = angle of diffraction (°)
  • \(n\) = order of the maximum
  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength of the light (m)

grating spacing

diffraction gratings are often specified by the number of lines per unit length.

if a grating has \(N\) lines per metre, the separation between adjacent slits is

$$\boxed{d=\frac{1}{N}}$$

\(d\) must be expressed in metres when using the diffraction grating equation.

orders of diffraction

the central maximum corresponds to the zeroth order, where \(n=0\). Higher-order maxima occur symmetrically on either side of the central maximum.

for the first order, \(n=1\); for the second order, \(n=2\); and so on.

higher orders occur at larger diffraction angles, provided that they are physically possible.

determining the wavelength of light

a diffraction grating can be used to determine the wavelength of monochromatic light by measuring the angle at which a particular diffraction order occurs.

the grating is placed in the path of the light and the bright diffraction maxima are observed. the angle \(\theta\) for a known order \(n\) is measured relative to the central maximum.

the wavelength is then calculated using

$$\boxed{\lambda=\frac{d\sin\theta}{n}}$$

experimental procedure

  1. place the diffraction grating in the path of the monochromatic light.
  2. identify the central maximum and the diffraction maxima on either side.
  3. measure the angle \(\theta\) for a known diffraction order \(n\).
  4. determine the grating spacing \(d\) from the number of lines per metre.
  5. calculate the wavelength using \(d\sin\theta=n\lambda\).

measuring maxima on both sides of the central maximum and using the mean angle can reduce experimental uncertainty.

using the equation

for a given grating and wavelength, increasing the diffraction order increases the angle \(\theta\).

a grating with more lines per metre has a smaller slit separation \(d\), producing larger diffraction angles for the same wavelength and order.

$$d\sin\theta=n\lambda$$

  • larger \(n\) → larger \(\theta\)
  • smaller \(d\) → larger \(\theta\)
  • larger \(\lambda\) → larger \(\theta\)

important limitation

the spectrometer itself is not included in this syllabus. the required understanding is limited to how a diffraction grating is used to determine the wavelength of light.

summary

  • a diffraction grating contains many equally spaced parallel slits.
  • light diffracts at each slit and the resulting waves interfere.
  • bright principal maxima occur when \(d\sin\theta=n\lambda\).
  • the slit separation is \(d=1/N\), where \(N\) is the number of lines per metre.
  • the wavelength of light can be determined by measuring \(\theta\) for a known diffraction order.
  • the structure and operation of a spectrometer are not required.
