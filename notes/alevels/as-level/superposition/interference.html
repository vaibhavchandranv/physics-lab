interference

interference

interference is the phenomenon that occurs when two or more waves superpose, producing a resultant wave whose amplitude depends on the relative phase of the waves.

constructive interference occurs when the waves arrive in phase, producing a maximum resultant amplitude.

destructive interference occurs when the waves arrive in antiphase, producing a minimum resultant amplitude.

$$\boxed{\text{constructive: path difference}=n\lambda}$$

$$\boxed{\text{destructive: path difference}=\left(n+\frac12\right)\lambda}$$

coherence

two sources are coherent if they produce waves of the same frequency with a constant phase difference.

coherent sources are required to produce a stable and observable interference pattern.

  • same frequency
  • constant phase difference

two-source interference

when waves from two coherent sources overlap, regions of constructive and destructive interference are produced.

these regions form an interference pattern consisting of alternating maxima and minima.

experiments

two-source interference can be demonstrated using different types of waves.

  • water waves: two coherent sources in a ripple tank produce alternating regions of constructive and destructive interference.
  • sound: two loudspeakers driven by the same signal can produce regions of loud and quiet sound.
  • light: coherent light sources can produce visible bright and dark fringes.
  • microwaves: two coherent microwave sources can produce regions of maximum and minimum intensity detected with a microwave detector.

conditions for observable fringes

a stable two-source interference pattern requires the sources to be coherent. they must have the same frequency and maintain a constant phase difference.

for light interference, the sources should also have the same polarisation and similar intensities so that the bright and dark fringes can be clearly observed.

double-slit interference

in a double-slit experiment, monochromatic light passes through two narrow slits. the two slits act as coherent sources because they are illuminated by the same wave.

the waves from the two slits overlap on a screen, producing a series of bright and dark fringes.

$$\boxed{\lambda=\frac{ax}{D}}$$

  • \(\lambda\) = wavelength of the light (m)
  • \(a\) = separation of the two slits (m)
  • \(x\) = fringe spacing (m)
  • \(D\) = distance from the slits to the screen (m)

the equation can be rearranged to determine any of the four quantities when the other three are known.

fringe spacing

the fringe spacing \(x\) is the distance between adjacent bright fringes or adjacent dark fringes.

from

$$\lambda=\frac{ax}{D}$$

the fringe spacing is

$$\boxed{x=\frac{\lambda D}{a}}$$

therefore, fringe spacing increases when the wavelength or screen distance increases, and decreases when the slit separation increases.

key idea

interference is a direct consequence of superposition. the pattern is stable only when the interfering sources maintain a constant phase relationship.

  • constructive interference → waves arrive in phase.
  • destructive interference → waves arrive in antiphase.
  • coherent sources have the same frequency and a constant phase difference.
  • double-slit interference produces alternating bright and dark fringes.
  • \(\lambda=ax/D\) relates wavelength, slit separation, fringe spacing and screen distance.

summary

  • interference occurs when waves superpose.
  • constructive interference produces maxima, while destructive interference produces minima.
  • coherent sources are required for a stable interference pattern.
  • two-source interference can be demonstrated using water, sound, light and microwaves.
  • for double-slit interference, \(\lambda=ax/D\).
