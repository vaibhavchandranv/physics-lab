8.2

diffraction

what is diffraction?

diffraction is the spreading of a wave when it passes through a gap or around an obstacle.

the amount of spreading depends strongly on the size of the gap relative to the wavelength of the wave.

diffraction is most significant when the gap width is comparable to the wavelength.

effect of gap width

when the gap is much wider than the wavelength, there is relatively little diffraction. the waves continue mainly in the original direction.

when the gap is similar in width to the wavelength, the waves spread out significantly after passing through the gap.

when the gap is narrower than the wavelength, the spreading is also substantial and the waves become approximately semicircular after passing through a narrow gap.

ripple tank experiment

diffraction can be demonstrated using water waves in a ripple tank. a barrier with a gap is placed in the path of the waves, and the wave pattern beyond the gap is observed.

the wavelength can be kept constant while the width of the gap is changed. the resulting wave patterns show how the degree of diffraction depends on the ratio of gap width to wavelength.

a narrow gap produces greater spreading, while a wide gap produces less spreading.

diffraction around an obstacle

waves can also diffract around the edges of an obstacle. the effect is most noticeable when the size of the obstacle is comparable to the wavelength.

diffraction does not require the wave to change speed or wavelength. it is a consequence of the wave spreading into regions that would otherwise be blocked.

key idea

the key factor determining the amount of diffraction is the ratio between the gap width and the wavelength:

$$\boxed{\text{greater diffraction when gap width}\sim\lambda}$$

  • gap width \(\gg\lambda\) → little diffraction
  • gap width \(\approx\lambda\) → significant diffraction
  • gap width \(<\lambda\) → strong spreading

summary

  • diffraction is the spreading of waves through gaps or around obstacles.
  • the amount of diffraction depends on the gap width relative to the wavelength.
  • diffraction is most noticeable when the gap width is comparable to the wavelength.
  • ripple tanks can be used to investigate diffraction using water waves.
  • a narrower gap generally produces greater spreading.
