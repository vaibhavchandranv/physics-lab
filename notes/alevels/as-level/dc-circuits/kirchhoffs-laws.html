10.2

kirchhoff's laws

kirchhoff's first law

kirchhoff's first law states that the total current entering a junction is equal to the total current leaving the junction.

$$\sum I_{\text{in}}=\sum I_{\text{out}}$$

this law is a consequence of the conservation of charge. charge cannot accumulate at a junction, so the amount of charge flowing in must equal the amount flowing out.

kirchhoff's second law

kirchhoff's second law states that the sum of the e.m.f.s around a complete loop is equal to the sum of the potential differences across the components in that loop.

$$\sum\mathcal{E}=\sum IR$$

this law is a consequence of the conservation of energy. the total energy supplied to charge carriers around a complete loop must equal the total energy transferred by the components.

resistors in series

when resistors are connected in series, the same current flows through every resistor.

applying kirchhoff's second law to a circuit containing two resistors gives

$$V=V_1+V_2$$

using $V=IR$ for each resistor,

$$IR_{\mathrm{total}}=IR_1+IR_2$$

therefore,

$$R_{\mathrm{total}}=R_1+R_2$$

for any number of resistors connected in series,

$$R_{\mathrm{total}}=R_1+R_2+R_3+\cdots$$

resistors in parallel

when resistors are connected in parallel, the potential difference across each resistor is the same.

applying kirchhoff's first law to a circuit containing two parallel resistors gives

$$I=I_1+I_2$$

using $I=\frac{V}{R}$ for each branch,

$$\frac{V}{R_{\mathrm{total}}} = \frac{V}{R_1}+\frac{V}{R_2}$$

cancelling $V$ gives

$$\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{total}}} = \frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}$$

for any number of resistors connected in parallel,

$$\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{total}}} = \frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}+\frac{1}{R_3}+\cdots$$

solving circuit problems

kirchhoff's laws can be used to determine unknown currents, potential differences, and resistances in circuits containing multiple loops or junctions.

first assign current directions to the different branches of the circuit. then apply kirchhoff's first law at junctions and kirchhoff's second law around independent loops.

if a calculated current is negative, the actual current flows in the opposite direction to the direction initially chosen.
