10.3

potential dividers

potential divider

a potential divider is a circuit containing resistors in series that produces a fraction of the supply potential difference across one of the resistors.

the same current flows through both resistors, so the potential difference across each resistor depends on its resistance.

$$V_{\mathrm{out}}=V_{\mathrm{in}}\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}$$

  • $V_{\mathrm{out}}$ = output potential difference (V)
  • $V_{\mathrm{in}}$ = supply potential difference (V)
  • $R_1$ = resistance of the first resistor ($\Omega$)
  • $R_2$ = resistance of the second resistor ($\Omega$)

potentiometer

a potentiometer is a variable potential divider that can be used to provide an adjustable potential difference.

it can also be used to compare potential differences by adjusting the output until it matches a known or reference potential difference.

null methods

a null method compares two potential differences by adjusting a circuit until there is no current through a sensitive meter.

a galvanometer is used to detect very small currents. when the galvanometer reads zero, the circuit is said to be at a null point.

at the null point, no current flows through the galvanometer, so it does not affect the potential difference being measured.

thermistors in potential dividers

a thermistor can be used in a potential divider to produce an output potential difference that depends on temperature.

for a thermistor with a negative temperature coefficient, its resistance decreases as temperature increases.

if the thermistor is used as the output resistor, an increase in temperature decreases its resistance and therefore decreases the output potential difference.

ldrs in potential dividers

a light-dependent resistor (LDR) can be used in a potential divider to produce an output potential difference that depends on light intensity.

the resistance of an LDR decreases as the light intensity increases.

if the LDR is used as the output resistor, increasing the light intensity decreases its resistance and therefore decreases the output potential difference.
