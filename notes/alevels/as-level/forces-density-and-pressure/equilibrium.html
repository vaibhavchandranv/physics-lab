equilibrium of forces

equilibrium

an object is said to be in equilibrium when it has neither linear acceleration nor rotational acceleration.

two conditions must be satisfied for complete equilibrium.

  • the resultant force acting on the object is zero
  • the resultant moment about any point is zero

$$\sum F = 0$$

$$\sum M = 0$$

principle of moments

for an object in rotational equilibrium, the total clockwise moments about any point equal the total anticlockwise moments about the same point.

$$\sum \text{clockwise moments}=\sum \text{anticlockwise moments}$$

  • the moments may be taken about any convenient point
  • choosing the pivot often simplifies calculations
  • only perpendicular distances are used

using the principle of moments

  1. choose a pivot.
  2. identify all forces acting on the object.
  3. calculate each clockwise moment.
  4. calculate each anticlockwise moment.
  5. equate the two sums and solve for the unknown.

remember that the distance used is always the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force.

balanced forces

when the resultant force acting on an object is zero, the forces are said to be balanced.

  • balanced forces do not change an object's state of motion
  • the object may remain at rest
  • or it may continue moving with constant velocity

however, balanced forces alone do not guarantee equilibrium if there is a resultant moment.

rotational equilibrium

an object is in rotational equilibrium when the resultant moment acting on it is zero.

  • clockwise and anticlockwise moments balance each other
  • the object does not rotate or change its rate of rotation

important notes

  • zero resultant force prevents linear acceleration.
  • zero resultant moment prevents rotational acceleration.
  • both conditions must be satisfied for complete equilibrium.
  • the principle of moments applies only when rotational equilibrium exists.
