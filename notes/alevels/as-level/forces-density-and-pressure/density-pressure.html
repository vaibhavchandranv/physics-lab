density and pressure

density

density is the mass contained in unit volume of a substance. it indicates how closely matter is packed.

$$\rho=\frac{m}{V}$$

  • SI unit: kg m-3
  • CGS unit: g cm-3
  • density is a scalar quantity.

substances with greater density have more mass in the same volume.

pressure

pressure is the normal force acting per unit area of a surface.

$$P=\frac{F}{A}$$

  • SI unit: pascal (Pa).
  • 1 Pa = 1 N m-2.
  • pressure is a scalar quantity.

for the same force, pressure increases when the area decreases.

atmospheric pressure

the air surrounding the earth exerts pressure due to its weight. this is called atmospheric pressure.

  • standard atmospheric pressure is 1.013 × 105 Pa.
  • measured using a barometer.
  • decreases with increase in altitude.

pascal's law

pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally and undiminished in all directions throughout the fluid.

  • applies only to enclosed fluids.
  • forms the basis of hydraulic machines.

applications

  • hydraulic lift
  • hydraulic press
  • hydraulic brake

archimedes' principle

when a body is wholly or partially immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.

$$\text{Buoyant Force}=\text{Weight of Fluid Displaced}$$

buoyancy

buoyancy is the upward force exerted by a fluid on an immersed object.

  • acts opposite to gravity.
  • depends on the density of the fluid.
  • depends on the volume of fluid displaced.

floatation

a body floats when the buoyant force acting on it equals its weight.

  • weight > buoyant force → object sinks.
  • weight = buoyant force → object floats.
  • weight < buoyant force → object rises until equilibrium is reached.

ships float because they displace a large volume of water, producing a buoyant force equal to their weight.

important notes

  • density is mass per unit volume.
  • pressure is force per unit area.
  • pressure in liquids increases with depth.
  • atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude.
  • pascal's law explains the working of hydraulic machines.
  • archimedes' principle explains buoyancy and floatation.
