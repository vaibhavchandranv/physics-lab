electric current

electric current

an electric current is a flow of charge carriers through a material.

in a metal, the charge carriers are free electrons. in other materials, the charge carriers may be ions or other particles.

quantisation of charge

electric charge is quantised, meaning that charge exists only in discrete amounts rather than arbitrary values.

the total charge is an integer multiple of the elementary charge.

$$Q=ne$$

  • \(Q\) = total charge (C)
  • \(n\) = number of charge carriers
  • \(e\) = elementary charge (\(1.60\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}\))

an electron has charge \(-e\), while a proton has charge \(+e\).

charge and current

electric current is the rate of flow of charge through a point in a circuit.

$$I=\frac{Q}{t}$$

  • \(I\) = current (A)
  • \(Q\) = charge transferred (C)
  • \(t\) = time (s)

rearranging gives

$$Q=It$$

one ampere is one coulomb of charge passing a point per second.

$$1\,\mathrm{A}=1\,\mathrm{C\,s^{-1}}$$

drift velocity

charge carriers in a conductor are in constant random motion due to thermal energy.

when an electric field is applied, the charge carriers acquire a small average velocity in one direction. this is called drift velocity.

in a metal, electrons drift opposite to the direction of conventional current because electrons have negative charge.

the drift velocity is much smaller than the random velocities of the electrons.

current in a conductor

the current in a conductor depends on the number density of charge carriers, the charge carried by each carrier, the cross-sectional area, and the drift velocity.

$$I= n e A v_d$$

  • \(I\) = current (A)
  • \(A\) = cross-sectional area (m2)
  • \(n\) = number density of charge carriers (m-3)
  • \(v_d\) = drift velocity (m s-1)
  • \(e\) = charge of each charge carrier (C)
