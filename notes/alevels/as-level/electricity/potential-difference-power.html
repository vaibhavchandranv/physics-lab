9.2

potential difference and power

potential difference

potential difference across a component is the energy transferred per unit charge passing through the component.

a larger potential difference means that more energy is transferred for each coulomb of charge.

$$V=\frac{W}{Q}$$

  • \(V\) = potential difference (V)
  • \(W\) = energy transferred (J)
  • \(Q\) = charge transferred (C)

one volt is equal to one joule of energy transferred per coulomb of charge.

$$1\,\mathrm{V}=1\,\mathrm{J\,C^{-1}}$$

energy transfer

when charge passes through a component, electrical energy is transferred to other forms of energy.

the energy transferred depends on the charge passing through the component and the potential difference across it.

$$W=VQ$$

  • \(W\) = energy transferred (J)
  • \(V\) = potential difference (V)
  • \(Q\) = charge transferred (C)

electrical power

electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred.

$$P=\frac{W}{t}$$

  • \(P\) = power (W)
  • \(W\) = energy transferred (J)
  • \(t\) = time (s)

since $W=VQ$ and $I=\frac{Q}{t}$, electrical power can be written as

$$P=VI$$

  • \(P\) = power (W)
  • \(V\) = potential difference (V)
  • \(I\) = current (A)

power and resistance

using ohm's law, the electrical power equations can also be written in terms of current and resistance or potential difference and resistance. substitute either V or I with their formulae from ohm's law to get these formulae.

$$P=I^2R$$

$$P=\frac{V^2}{R}$$

  • \(P\) = power (W)
  • \(I\) = current (A)
  • \(V\) = potential difference (V)
  • \(R\) = resistance (\(\Omega\))
