4.2

electrical quantities

4.2.1 electric charge

charge

electric charge is a property of matter that causes electric forces. charge can be positive or negative.

  • unit: coulomb (c)
  • like charges repel, unlike charges attract
  • charge is conserved
  • charge is carried by electrons in metals

charging by friction: electrons are transferred from one material to another.

electric field: region in which an electric charge experiences a force.

direction of electric field: direction of force on a positive charge.

4.2.2 electric current

current

electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge.

  • symbol: i
  • unit: ampere (a)
  • equation: i = q / t
  • conventional current flows from positive to negative

direct current (d.c.): current that flows in one direction only.

alternating current (a.c.): current that changes direction repeatedly.

4.2.3 electromotive force and potential difference

potential difference (p.d.)

potential difference is the energy transferred per unit charge between two points in a circuit.

  • symbol: v
  • unit: volt (v)
  • equation: v = w / q
  • measured using a voltmeter in parallel

electromotive force (emf)

emf is the energy supplied per unit charge by a source.

  • symbol: e
  • unit: volt (v)
  • measured across a source when no current flows

4.2.4 resistance

resistance

resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current.

  • symbol: r
  • unit: ohm (Ω)
  • equation: r = v / i

factors affecting resistance

  • length of conductor (longer = greater resistance)
  • cross-sectional area (thicker = lower resistance)
  • material of conductor
  • temperature (for metals, resistance increases with temperature)

ohm’s law

for a conductor at constant temperature, current is directly proportional to potential difference.

v = ir

4.2.5 electrical energy and electrical power

electrical power

electrical power is the rate of transfer of electrical energy.

  • symbol: p
  • unit: watt (w)
  • equations: p = vi, p = i²r, p = v² / r

electrical energy

electrical energy is the total energy transferred in a circuit.

  • unit: joule (j)
  • equation: e = vit
  • commercial unit: kilowatt-hour (kwh)
