1.5

forces

1.5.1 | effects of forces

changes in size and shape

forces can cause compression, extension, deformation, or permanent changes.

load–extension graphs

linear region obeys hooke's law, then non-linear beyond limit of proportionality.

resultant force

vector sum of forces along same line. equilibrium when: $F_{resultant} = 0$

newton's first law

object stays at rest or constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force.

changes in velocity

  • changing direction (perpendicular force)
  • changing speed (parallel force)

spring constant

  • $k = \frac{F}{x}$ (N/m)
  • $F = kx$ (hooke's law)

limit of proportionality is where graph stops being linear.

newton's second law

$F = ma$

friction

solid friction: opposes motion between surfaces.

liquid drag: increases with speed and viscosity.

air resistance: proportional to $v^2$.

1.5.2 | turning effect of forces

moment of a force is turning effect about a pivot.

moment definition

$M = F \times d$ (Nm)

equilibrium

sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments.

experimental verification: balance meter rule using weights.

1.5.3 | centre of gravity

centre of gravity is point where entire weight acts.

finding it

hang object, use plumb line, repeat from different points. intersection gives centre.

stability

  • lower centre of gravity → more stable
  • higher → less stable
  • wide base → more stable
  • line of action must fall within base

line of action = vertical line through centre of gravity.
